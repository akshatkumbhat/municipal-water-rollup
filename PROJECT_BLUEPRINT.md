# Project Copperline
## Municipal Water & Wastewater Asset-Integrity Services Buy-and-Build

## Investment objective

Build a long-duration, technology-enabled field-services compounder serving municipal water, wastewater, and stormwater systems. Acquire a $7M-$15M revenue anchor with dispatch density, management depth, and recurring municipal programs; then consolidate founder-owned inspection, cleaning, leak detection, valve/hydrant, sampling, and asset-data businesses in adjacent geographies.

This is a service rollup, not an investment in regulated utility ownership. The underwriting case rests on recurring compliance and maintenance work, local reputation, licensed/certified labor, route density, data standardization, and disciplined tuck-in pricing.

# Phase 1 — Outbound sourcing and data engineering

## Target profile

### Anchor platform

- Revenue: $7M-$15M; modeled case is $10M.
- EBITDA: $1.4M-$3.5M; normalized margin of 18%-25%.
- Mix: at least 60% municipal/public-works customers; at least 50% recurring or programmatic revenue.
- Services: two or more of CCTV/condition assessment, cleaning/jetting, leak detection, valve/hydrant programs, I&I diagnostics, utility locating, compliance sampling, or asset-data services.
- Field organization: 15-60 technicians/operators; a dispatcher or operations manager below the founder.
- Geography: dense two-to-four-hour service radius with adjacent expansion markets.
- Customer concentration: no customer above 20% of revenue; top ten below 55%.
- Capex: maintenance capex below 4% of revenue excluding identifiable growth fleet.
- Quality gates: NASSCO or relevant state credentials, clean safety record, defensible bid history, no dependence on one equipment OEM, and a credible second layer of management.

### Add-on

- Revenue: $1M-$5M.
- EBITDA: 15%-25% normalized margin.
- Field organization: 5-25 technicians/operators.
- Geography: within or immediately adjacent to an existing branch radius.
- Strategic purpose: route density, customer access, skilled labor, a new service line, or a tuck-in branch.
- Founder transition: willing to remain 12-24 months or provide a defined handoff.

### Automatic exclusions

- Commodity-heavy civil construction with bid-day economics and limited service revenue.
- Regulated utility ownership.
- Equipment manufacturing or distribution without field-service pull-through.
- Environmental remediation with material unknown liabilities.
- Businesses with unresolved licensing, safety, wage-and-hour, or public-contracting issues.

## Sourcing funnel

1. Collect names from public trade-association directories, approved-contractor lists, public bid awards, state license files, and regional water-industry directories.
2. Normalize names, domains, addresses, phones, and service keywords.
3. Enrich company websites for age, employee/technician/fleet references, service mix, contactability, and digital footprint.
4. Deduplicate by domain and normalized company name.
5. Score 0-100 and assign a separate data-confidence score.
6. Hand-research the top 15; call the top five.
7. Log every touch, owner note, succession signal, and reason for pass in the CRM.

## Lead score

### Company age — 35 points

- Under 5 years: 4
- 5-9 years: 10
- 10-19 years: 22
- 20-39 years: 30
- 40+ years: 35
- Unknown: 10, with lower data confidence

### Field workforce fit — 40 points

Use technician count when available; otherwise estimate technicians as 65% of employees.

- Fewer than 3 technicians: 7
- 3-7: 20-28, scaled linearly
- 8-35: 40
- 36-60: declines from 40 to 30
- 61-100: declines from 30 to 20
- Above 100: 15
- Unknown: 12, with lower data confidence

The score favors companies large enough to have transferable operations but still small enough to be founder-owned and actionable.

### Digital whitespace — 25 points

- Working website: 5
- HTTPS: 2
- Phone visible: 2
- Email visible: 2
- Contact/quote path: 2
- Two to 15 internal pages: 7; 16-40 pages: 5; above 40: 2
- Zero to two social profiles: 5; three to four: 3; five or more: 1

A credible but under-optimized web presence is treated as a sourcing advantage. The score does not reward maximum marketing sophistication.

## CRM fields

- Company and URL

- Owner/founder and estimated age/tenure
- Founding year and company age
- Employee, technician, crew, and fleet estimates
- Service lines and certifications
- Municipal/industrial/commercial mix

## Outreach cadence

- Day 1: short owner email and call.
- Day 4: second call with one specific observation about the business.
- Day 10: physical letter or concise partnership note.
- Day 21: final call and permission-based closeout.
- Quarterly: thesis update, relevant acquisition announcement, or local-market insight.

The message should emphasize permanent capital, operating autonomy, employee continuity, and the ability to invest in fleet, systems, and management—not a rapid flip.

# Phase 2 — Buy-and-build financial model

## Base transaction assumptions

### Platform at close

- Revenue: $10.0M
- EBITDA margin: 20.0%
- EBITDA: $2.0M
- Entry multiple: 6.0x EBITDA
- Enterprise value: $12.0M
- Transaction fees: 2.0% of EV, or $0.24M
- Initial debt: 3.0x EBITDA, or $6.0M
- Initial sponsor equity: $6.24M

### Add-ons

| Add-on | Close year | Revenue | EBITDA margin | EBITDA | Entry multiple | EV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 2 | $2.0M | 18% | $0.36M | 3.5x | $1.26M |
| B | 3 | $2.5M | 18% | $0.45M | 3.5x | $1.58M |
| C | 4 | $3.0M | 20% | $0.60M | 3.5x | $2.10M |

Add-ons are modeled as debt-funded at close because the platform's annual cash sweep keeps pro-forma leverage below 2.5x in the case. In a live transaction, financing should be sized to covenant headroom and integration capacity, not to maximize headline IRR.

## Operating assumptions

- Organic growth: 5.0% annually.
- Platform margin expansion: 50 bps per year, capped at 22.0%.
- Add-on SG&A: 20% of revenue.
- SG&A consolidation: 15%, equal to 3% of add-on revenue at full run rate.
- Synergy realization: 50% in the acquisition year, 100% thereafter.
- Interest rate: 8.0%.
- Maintenance capex: 3.0% of revenue.
- Cash taxes: 25% of EBITDA less maintenance capex and cash interest.
- NWC investment: 1.0% of incremental revenue.
- Cash sweep: 100% of levered free cash flow.
- Year-five valuation mark: 6.5x EBITDA.

## Value-creation bridge

1. Organic pricing and volume growth.
2. Add-on EBITDA acquired below the platform multiple.
3. Centralized finance, insurance, software, dispatch, recruiting, and procurement.
4. Route density and technician utilization.
5. Better contract renewal, cross-selling, and customer coverage.
6. Debt reduction through free cash flow.
7. Modest re-rating from a local founder business to a scaled multi-branch platform.

## IC guardrails

- No add-on should be approved solely because it is multiple-arbitrage accretive.
- Underwrite synergies by named cost center, owner, timing, and one-time cost.
- Separate maintenance fleet capex from growth fleet capex.
- Test prevailing-wage, public-contract, safety, environmental, licensing, and union exposure.
- Run concentration by customer, municipality, contract type, branch, service line, and technician certification.
- Run a downside case with 3% growth, half synergy capture, 9% interest, and a 6.0x valuation mark.

# Phase 3 — Operations and KPI dashboard

## KPI architecture

### 1. Route density

Completed jobs per 100 route miles. This links branch geography, dispatch quality, fuel, windshield time, and capacity utilization.

### 2. Technician billable utilization

Billable field hours divided by paid field hours. Review by branch, service line, dispatcher, and technician cohort.

### 3. Gross margin by service line

Revenue less direct labor, materials, disposal, and subcontractors. This exposes pricing leakage, job-estimating errors, rework, and low-quality revenue.

### 4. Recurring revenue mix

Revenue from recurring inspection, cleaning, sampling, monitoring, and maintenance programs divided by total revenue.

### 5. Customer churn / gross revenue retention

Lost customers or lost recurring revenue divided by the opening customer or recurring-revenue base. Track municipal renewals separately from transactional accounts.

### 6. Free cash flow conversion

EBITDA less maintenance capex, cash taxes, cash interest, and working-capital investment, divided by EBITDA. Pair it with DSO because municipal receivables can obscure operating quality.

## 100-day operating plan

### Days 0-30: establish control

- Freeze the chart of accounts and define branch/service-line mappings.
- Reconcile technician roster, fleet, certifications, customer master, contracts, backlog, and AR aging.
- Install weekly cash, bookings, labor, utilization, and safety reporting.
- Identify top 20 customers and all contracts renewing within 180 days.

### Days 31-60: remove leakage

- Standardize quoting and job costing.
- Reprice unprofitable service lines and emergency callout terms.
- Centralize insurance, software, telecom, recruiting, and selected procurement.
- Pilot dispatch clustering and technician scheduling in one branch.
- Create a collections cadence by municipality and invoice status.

### Days 61-100: build the compounding system

- Launch cross-sell playbooks by installed customer base.
- Create certification and technician recruiting funnels.
- Implement branch scorecards and manager incentives.
- Build a target map around existing branches and customer adjacencies.
- Define integration templates for finance, HR, safety, dispatch, customer data, and brand architecture.

## Weekly operating cadence

- Monday: safety, staffing, schedule, route density, backlog.
- Wednesday: quote pipeline, pricing, job margin exceptions, AR blockers.
- Friday: cash, EBITDA bridge, customer renewals, integration actions.
- Monthly: branch review, service-line P&L, technician cohort metrics, and M&A capacity.

## Candidate deliverable standard

A strong submission should arrive with:

- A reproducible 50+ company sourcing database.
- A top-15 target list with five hand-researched owner profiles.
- A transparent model with assumptions, formulas, and a downside case.
- A live dashboard with sample data and upload capability.
- A concise investment committee memo and a five-minute product demonstration.
